# Gaggia Classic: Temperature Surfing and the PID Mod

*Source: Home-Barista.com Gaggia Classic owners thread (paraphrased)*

The Gaggia Classic Pro uses a single boiler with a simple thermostat, so the boiler temperature swings up and down around the brew setpoint. Because the thermostat has a wide dead band, the water can be anywhere from too cool to too hot depending on where in the heating cycle you pull the shot. Espresso wants water around **92–96°C (about 198–205°F)** at the puck.

**Temperature surfing** is the manual technique to land in that window without a PID:

1. Turn the machine on and let it fully heat until the brew (heating) light turns OFF.
2. Wait about 30–60 seconds after the light goes off so the group head soaks up heat.
3. Flush a short burst of water through the group, then wait for the heating light to cycle ON and then OFF again.
4. Pull your shot in the window right after the light clicks off — the boiler is at a repeatable temperature at that moment.

This gives you a consistent starting temperature shot-to-shot. It's fiddly, which is why the most popular Gaggia Classic upgrade is the **PID mod**: adding a PID controller that holds the boiler within ~1°C of a target you set (many people use 93–94°C for brew). With a PID you skip surfing entirely and just pull.

Steam mode uses a separate higher thermostat; don't try to brew right after steaming without running water through to cool the boiler back to brew temperature, or you'll scorch the shot.

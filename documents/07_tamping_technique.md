# Tamping Technique: Pressure, Level, and Why It Matters Less Than You Think

*Source: r/espresso and barista forum consensus (paraphrased)*

Beginners obsess over tamping pressure, but the community consensus is that **levelness matters far more than force**. The job of the tamp is to compress the grounds into an even, flat puck with no gaps — not to crush them with maximum strength.

Guidelines:

- **Use a tamper that matches your basket diameter** (54mm for Breville, 58mm for most prosumer machines, 58mm for Gaggia Classic Pro). A loose-fitting tamper leaves an un-compressed ring at the edge where water channels.
- **Tamp level.** Press straight down; a tilted tamp makes one side denser than the other and causes channeling. Many people check by eye or use a self-leveling/spring-loaded (calibrated) tamper.
- **Pressure is not critical above a threshold.** Roughly 10–15 kg (about 20–30 lb) is plenty. Past the point where the grounds stop compressing, more force does almost nothing. Consistency shot-to-shot matters more than the exact number.
- **Don't "polish" or tap the side after tamping** — tapping can crack the puck and create channels. Just one firm level press.

A calibrated/spring tamper that clicks at a set pressure removes the variable entirely and is a cheap upgrade for consistency. But if your shots channel, fix distribution (WDT) before blaming your tamp — uneven grounds under a perfect tamp still channel.

# Fixing Channeling: WDT and Puck Prep

*Source: r/espresso puck-prep discussions (paraphrased)*

**Channeling** is when water finds a low-resistance path through the espresso puck and blasts through one spot instead of soaking the whole puck evenly. You'll see it as a spurting/spritzing stream, blonde fast jets, or a wet crater on the spent puck. Channeling causes simultaneous over- and under-extraction, so the shot tastes both harsh and sour and is impossible to dial in consistently.

The root cause is uneven density in the puck — usually **clumps** from the grinder and uneven distribution. The fix is better puck preparation, in this order:

1. **WDT (Weiss Distribution Technique):** stir the dry grounds in the basket with a set of thin needles (a "WDT tool"). This breaks up clumps and creates an even, fluffy bed. This is the single highest-impact anti-channeling step.
2. **Use a dosing funnel** (or dosing ring) on top of the basket so grounds don't spill on the rim and so you can WDT without mess.
3. **Distribute/level** the surface flat — a distribution tool or gentle tap settles it evenly.
4. **Tamp level and consistent.** A tilted tamp creates a thin side that channels.

Other channeling causes: too little coffee (low dose leaves headspace, puck cracks), a chipped/dirty basket, or grinding too fine so the puck can't take the water evenly. If WDT alone doesn't fix it, check your dose and grind next. Polishing/tap-to-settle tricks help less than WDT — fix clumps first.

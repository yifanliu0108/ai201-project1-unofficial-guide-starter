# Sour vs Bitter: The Grind/Taste Troubleshooting Loop

*Source: Home-Barista.com forum thread "Why does my espresso taste sour?" (paraphrased)*

New users constantly confuse sour and bitter. They are opposite problems with opposite fixes, so getting the diagnosis right saves you a lot of wasted coffee.

**Sour = acidic, like biting a lemon, makes you wince.** This is under-extraction. The water passed through the puck too quickly and only grabbed the easy-to-dissolve acids before the sugars and pleasant compounds came out. Causes and fixes:

- Grind too coarse → **grind finer** (the main fix).
- Dose too low → add a gram or two of coffee.
- Water too cool → raise brew temperature if your machine allows it.
- Shot pulled too short → let more liquid out (higher ratio).

**Bitter = harsh, dry, lingering, ashy.** This is over-extraction. The water sat in the puck too long and dissolved the bitter compounds that come out last. Causes and fixes:

- Grind too fine → **grind coarser**.
- Dose too high for the basket → reduce dose slightly.
- Water too hot → lower brew temperature.
- Shot pulled too long → stop the shot earlier (lower ratio).

A shot that pulls **too fast and tastes sour** is the most common beginner complaint. The answer is almost always **grind finer** so the water meets more resistance and spends more time extracting. Change grind one step at a time and re-taste; do not change grind, dose, and temperature all at once or you'll never know which fixed it.

Tip: a balanced shot has both a bright note and a sweet finish. If you only fix sourness until it goes bitter, back off one step — the sweet spot is just before bitterness.

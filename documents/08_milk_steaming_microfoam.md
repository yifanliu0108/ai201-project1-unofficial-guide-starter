# Steaming Milk for Microfoam and Latte Art

*Source: r/latteart beginner guide (paraphrased)*

Microfoam is milk textured into a glossy, paint-like consistency with no large visible bubbles — the base for latte art. The two phases of steaming are **stretching** (adding air) and **texturing** (rolling to break bubbles into fine foam).

Steps:

1. Start with **cold milk** in a cold pitcher, filled to just below the spout (about 1/3 to 1/2 full). Cold milk gives you more time before it overheats.
2. **Purge the steam wand** before inserting it to clear condensation.
3. Position the tip just under the surface and open steam fully. You'll hear a gentle "paper tearing" hiss as air is introduced — this is **stretching**. Do this only in the first few seconds while the milk is cold.
4. Once you've added enough air (the pitcher volume rises slightly), submerge the tip a bit deeper and angle it to create a **whirlpool**. This is **texturing** — it folds the big bubbles into microfoam.
5. Stop at about **60–65°C (140–150°F)** — too hot scalds the milk and kills sweetness. If you don't have a thermometer, stop when the pitcher is uncomfortably hot to hold for more than a couple seconds.
6. Tap the pitcher to pop big bubbles and swirl continuously to keep the foam glossy until you pour.

Whole milk steams the most forgivingly because fat and protein stabilize the foam. A single-hole steam tip (see the Silvia wand mod) makes a tighter whirlpool than a panarello, which is why it's preferred for art.

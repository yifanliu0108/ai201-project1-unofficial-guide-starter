# Backflushing, Cleaning, and Descaling

*Source: Home-Barista maintenance threads (paraphrased)*

Two different maintenance tasks get confused: **backflushing** (cleaning coffee oils from the group) and **descaling** (removing mineral scale from the boiler). They use different chemicals and you should never mix them up.

**Backflushing** — only on machines with a **three-way solenoid valve** (e.g., Gaggia Classic, most prosumer machines; the Breville Bambino does *not* have one and should not be backflushed with a blank).

- *Water backflush:* put a **blind/blank basket** (no holes) in the portafilter, lock in, run the pump a few seconds, release, repeat. This pushes water back through the group and flushes the solenoid.
- *Detergent backflush:* add a small amount of espresso machine cleaner (e.g., Cafiza) to the blind basket and repeat the cycle, then rinse thoroughly with several water-only backflushes. Do this **weekly** for daily users.

**Descaling** — removes calcium scale from the boiler caused by hard water.

- Use a descaling solution or citric acid per the machine manual; run it through the boiler and steam circuit, then rinse extensively.
- Frequency depends on water hardness — every 1–3 months on hard water, less on soft/filtered water. Using low-mineral water dramatically reduces how often you descale.
- **Never** put descaler in a blind basket to backflush, and never put backflush detergent in the water tank to descale. Different jobs, different chemicals.

Daily: wipe the steam wand and purge it after every use; rinse the group and portafilter; knock out the puck. A clean machine tastes better and channels less.

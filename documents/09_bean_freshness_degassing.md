# Bean Freshness, Degassing, and the Resting Window

*Source: r/espresso and roaster blog discussions (paraphrased)*

Fresh coffee is the cheapest upgrade to your espresso, but **too fresh** is also a problem for espresso specifically. After roasting, beans release CO2 — this is **degassing**. Very fresh beans (1–4 days off roast) are so gassy that the puck bubbles and erupts under pressure, causing gushers, channeling, and a thin, sour shot that's impossible to dial in.

The community resting window for espresso:

- **Wait at least 7–10 days after the roast date** before pulling espresso. Many people find the sweet spot is **10–21 days** off roast.
- Espresso wants more rest than filter/pour-over because the 9-bar pressure exaggerates CO2 problems.
- The "best by" date on supermarket bags is not a roast date and usually means the beans are months old and stale. Buy from a roaster that prints the **roast date**.

Storage: keep beans in an **airtight, opaque container at room temperature**, away from heat and light. Do **not** store beans in the fridge — humidity and odors ruin them. You *can* freeze beans long-term in airtight portions, but never refreeze, and let them come to room temperature before grinding.

Symptoms of stale beans (past ~5–6 weeks): no crema, flat lifeless taste, and the grind needs to keep getting finer to slow the shot. Symptoms of too-fresh beans: erupting puck, gushing shots, and grind settings that won't stabilize day to day.

# Pressurized vs Non-Pressurized Baskets (Single vs Dual Wall)

*Source: r/espresso basket explainer threads (paraphrased)*

Entry machines (Breville, De'Longhi, Gaggia bundles) often ship with **pressurized** baskets, and beginners don't realize their machine came with one. Knowing the difference explains a lot of "my crema looks great but it tastes bad" confusion.

**Pressurized (dual-wall) baskets** have a second wall with a single tiny pinhole. They create artificial backpressure regardless of grind, so they produce thick foamy "crema" even with coarse, stale, pre-ground coffee. They are forgiving — great if you don't have a good grinder. But the crema is fake (mostly aerated bubbles), and because the basket controls the pressure, **changing your grind barely changes the shot** — you can't truly dial in.

**Non-pressurized (single-wall) baskets** have a normal perforated bottom. The puck itself provides the resistance, so the shot quality depends entirely on grind, dose, distribution, and tamp. These are what you want for real espresso — but they are **unforgiving** and require a **burr grinder** capable of fine, espresso-range grinding. Blade grinders and most cheap grinders cannot grind fine/consistent enough for a single-wall basket and will only gush or choke.

Rule of thumb: if your "crema" is huge and bubbly but the espresso tastes thin or off, and adjusting grind does nothing, you're probably on a pressurized basket. Switch to single-wall once you have a real grinder. You can tell them apart by flipping the basket over — a pressurized one has a solid back with one pinhole; a non-pressurized one is perforated all the way through.

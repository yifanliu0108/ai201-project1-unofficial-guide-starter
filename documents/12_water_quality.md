# Water Quality for Espresso

*Source: Home-Barista water chemistry threads (paraphrased)*

Espresso is ~90% water, and water chemistry affects both taste and your machine's lifespan. There are two concerns: **flavor** (extraction) and **scale** (machine protection).

Key ideas the community agrees on:

- **Don't use straight distilled or zero-TDS RO water** for taste — water with no minerals extracts flatly and tastes hollow, and some boilers with water-level probes won't even register pure water. Minerals (especially magnesium and some calcium) actively help extract flavor.
- **Don't use hard tap water** either — high calcium/bicarbonate causes **scale** buildup in the boiler that eventually clogs and damages the machine, and high alkalinity mutes acidity and makes shots taste dull/flat.
- The target many people cite is roughly **50–150 ppm (mg/L) total hardness** with **low alkalinity**. The SCA recommends around 75–125 ppm total dissolved solids as a target.
- Practical options: a good **carbon/ion filter** (Brita-type) on moderate tap water; bottled water in the right range (people cite low-mineral spring waters); or **remineralized RO water** for full control. Some enthusiasts mix distilled water with a measured mineral concentrate (e.g., "Third Wave Water" packets) to hit a target recipe exactly.

Using appropriately soft, low-alkalinity water means you **descale far less often** and your shots taste cleaner and brighter. If your shots taste flat and your machine is in a hard-water area, water is a likely culprit before grind.

# Dialing In Your First Espresso — Beginner Guide

*Source: r/espresso "Start Here" wiki + community FAQ (paraphrased from r/espresso/wiki/beginner)*

Dialing in means adjusting your grind, dose, and shot time until the espresso tastes balanced — not sour, not bitter. Almost every new home barista gets frustrated here. The single most important variable is **grind size**, and you should only change one thing at a time.

The two diagnostic flavors to memorize:

- **Sour, sharp, thin, watery** = the shot is **under-extracted**. The water rushed through too fast and didn't pull enough out of the grounds. Fix: **grind finer**. A finer grind packs the puck tighter, slows the water down, and raises extraction.
- **Bitter, harsh, dry, ashy** = the shot is **over-extracted**. The water moved too slowly and pulled out the bitter compounds. Fix: **grind coarser**.

A reliable starting recipe for a double shot is **18 grams of coffee in, 36 grams of espresso out, in 25 to 30 seconds**. This is the classic 1:2 ratio. Weigh both your dose (dry grounds) and your yield (liquid espresso) with a scale — eyeballing is the number one beginner mistake.

If your shot finishes in under 20 seconds it is running too fast — grind finer. If it takes over 35 seconds and drips slowly, it is running too slow — grind coarser. Adjust the grinder one or two clicks/steps at a time, because grind changes have a large effect.

Don't chase perfection on stale supermarket coffee. Fresh beans (roasted within the last 2–4 weeks) behave far more predictably and are worth more than any equipment upgrade at the start.
